Findings

  1. 26% of the exported rows were not usable as independent votes. 94,321 were repeat submissions of an image by the same volunteer (77,086 within 2 seconds, i.e. double submits on the old site) and 11,260 carried no answer. The dashboard's "Answers" count overstates the real number of classifications.
  2. How a skipped question is read changes the results more than anything else. 65% of yes/no votes answer only one of the nine questions, and 93% of those single answers are a yes: volunteers click the feature they see. Read as unknown, skips inflate the yes share (Spiral 72%, Smooth 66%); read as no, it is Spiral 35%, Smooth 25%. The 34,082 votes that answered all nine (no skips) give Spiral 38%, Smooth 37%: much closer to skipped = no, which slightly under-counts. See the comparison below.
  3. Some features are clear-cut, others contested (if skipped = unknown). Interacting and Mergers are decided (confident yes or no) for 70% and 63% of images, but only 44% for Galaxy-Group, 51% for Bar and 54% for Smooth. If skipped = no, 83%-100% of images are decided for every feature, almost all as "no".
  4. Spiral and Smooth are the most common features either way. Confidently spiral: 945 images (skipped = unknown) or 501 (skipped = no); confidently smooth: 784 or 190.
  5. The labels behave physically sensibly under both readings. Across images, the strongest opposite pair is Spiral vs Smooth (correlation -0.62; -0.65 if skipped = no) and the strongest pair that goes together is Interacting and Mergers (+0.85; +0.87).
  6. Volunteers skip most questions. In the yes/no form, each feature is left unanswered by 52%-77% of voters (Spiral is skipped least). Making all nine questions required in the form would remove the need to choose how to read a skip in future data.
  7. The form itself changed the answers. On the 1003 images classified in both forms, Smooth was mentioned by 14% of tick-box voters but 26% of yes/no voters. Tick-box and yes/no data should not be pooled naively; the outputs keep them apart.
  8. The free text adds a morphology the form lacks. 6,293 votes name a class in the "not listed" box: Elliptical 4,062, Irregular 1,120, Lenticular 891, Spiral-class 220. Elliptical is by far the most requested missing option; adding Elliptical/Lenticular/Irregular to the form would capture it.
  9. Volunteers are mostly reliable. Volunteers with 30+ scored answers agree with the clear majority 86% of the time (median); 40 of 433 agree less than half the time. Their votes are kept but carry less weight in p_yes_weighted.
  10. A small core does most of the work. The most active 10% of volunteers cast 62% of all votes; 47% of volunteers took part on a single day only.
  11. Training-set readiness. 2,003 of 2,130 images have at least 20 yes/no votes. Hard yes/no labels are scarce for rare features (confident "yes" images, unknown/no: Interacting 86/0, Mergers 89/8, Tidal-Tails 127/17, Bar 237/24), so train on the vote fractions (soft targets) rather than labels. training_images.csv has both readings: *_p_yes and *_p_yes_skip_no.

Skipped questions: read as "unknown" or as "no"?

In the yes/no form a volunteer may skip any of the nine questions. 65% of the 192,581 yes/no votes answer only one, and 93% of those single answers are a yes: people click the feature they see. The rest of this report reads a skip as unknown; this section shows what changes if a skip is read as no. The 34,082 votes that answered all nine questions skipped nothing and serve as the reference.

Consequences

  • Yes shares. Read as unknown, the share answering yes is 13-34 points above the no-skip reference (votes that answered all nine). Read as no, it is -12 to -3 points from it: a small under-count instead of a large over-count.
  • Labels. Read as unknown, 7,343 image-feature pairs are uncertain; read as no, 999. Almost every image becomes decided, mostly as "no". Confident "yes" images: Spiral 945 → 501, Smooth 784 → 190, Edge-on 380 → 157, Bar 237 → 24, Interacting 86 → 0.
  • Evidence per image. Read as no, every vote counts for every feature, so the median number of answers behind a feature on an image rises from 26 to 91 and the intervals narrow.
  • What stays the same. Which features go together (heatmaps), the cleaning counts, the form comparison, free-text morphology, activity and demographics do not depend on this choice.
  • What would also change if adopted. Volunteer agreement scores and flags (computed here with skipped = unknown), the weighted p_yes, and the site's clean_image_consensus table.
  • For machine learning. Both readings are in training_images.csv (*_p_yes and *_p_yes_skip_no). Vote fractions as soft targets suit either; hard labels from skipped = no are nearly all "no" for rare features.
